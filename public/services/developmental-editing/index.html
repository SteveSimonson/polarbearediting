Developmental editing for novels & narrative nonfiction

Big-picture book editing for plot, structure, character, pacing, and reader experience—before you polish every sentence.

Developmental editing is the cold-light pass: we look at whether the book works as a reading experience, not only whether the commas behave. If structure, stakes, or argument are soft, no amount of copyediting will save the reader’s afternoon.

This is not a promise of agents, lists, or sales. It is a promise of a prioritized revision you can actually execute—without sanding your voice off the page.

The process

  1. Fit check. Genre, word count, stage (draft two, post-beta, pre-agent), and what you already know is weak. If we are the wrong desk, we say so.
  2. Sample pages. A stretch from the opening and a stretch from the middle. Chemistry and the quality of comments matter more than a pretty rate card.
  3. Written scope. Deliverables, exclusions, timeline, price, and what happens if word count moves. No vague “full edit.”
  4. The read. The whole manuscript, twice if the book needs it: once as a reader, once as a structuralist with a pencil.
  5. Delivery and debrief. Letter, comments as scoped, a revision sequence, and space to ask “which fire first?”

What we examine

  • Structure & pacing — act turns, chapter propulsion, sagging middles, false endings
  • Character & point of view — desire, agency, voice consistency, viewpoint leaks
  • Plot logic & stakes — causality, coincidence debt, promise/payoff
  • Theme & tone — intentional resonance vs. accidental muddle
  • Market fit (optional) — genre expectations without sanding off your voice

Who it is for

Fiction (especially character-driven and genre novels), memoir, and narrative nonfiction. Indie, hybrid, and pre-agent authors who have revised as far as they can see—and who are ready to act on feedback, not only collect it.

If your project is primarily academic or technical documentation, we will refer you rather than stretch the brand. If you only need language polish and trusted readers already confirm the story lands, skip this pass and look at copyediting.

What you receive

  • An editorial letter prioritizing the highest-leverage revisions
  • In-manuscript comments on structural moments (scope-dependent)
  • A revision roadmap: sequence of fixes so you do not rewrite in circles
  • A debrief path so the letter does not sit in a drawer

We do not ghostwrite chapters, invent a commercial ending you did not earn, or claim the book will place with a house. Your name stays on the spine. Our job is to make the pages hold.

When not to buy this

  • The draft is not finished, or you already plan to cut a third of it
  • You have not done a serious self-revision (you will pay us to find what a cooling-off reread would have found)
  • You actually want commas, continuity, and a style sheet—that is copyediting
  • You want a letter, not markup, and a lighter diagnostic will get you moving—start with a manuscript critique
  • You want reassurance more than change. Hire when you are willing to break scenes.

Best time to book: after you have revised as far as you can on your own—ideally after trusted beta readers—and before a full copyedit. See when to hire an editor and the side-by-side developmental vs copyediting guide.

Educational rates

Industry-style ballparks for developmental editing typically run $0.03–$0.06 per word. A developmental-plus-copy package often lands around $0.045–$0.08 per word. FAQ

Is developmental editing the same as a critique?

A critique diagnoses; a developmental edit goes deeper with more granular guidance and, when scoped, manuscript markup. Many authors start with a critique to test fit.

Will you rewrite my book?

No. We guide decisions and model solutions. Your voice stays yours—the goal is a stronger manuscript you can still claim.

How much does developmental editing cost?

Educational market ranges for developmental editing typically run $0.03–$0.06 per word, depending on length, genre complexity, and manuscript condition. A combined developmental-plus-copy package often lands around $0.045–$0.08 per word. Use the cost calculator for a ballpark, then request a scoped quote.

Do I need beta readers before a developmental edit?

Not required, but they make the pass cheaper and sharper. If trusted readers already agree on the same three problems, we can spend more time on solutions and less time discovering the map.

How long does a developmental edit take?

Most trade-length novels are scheduled in weeks, not overnight. Timeline depends on word count, queue, and how much structural work the sample reveals. The written scope names a delivery date before you pay a deposit.
